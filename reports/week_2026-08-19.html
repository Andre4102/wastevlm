Leakage was re-verified before any of these were quoted: MD5 + perceptual hash (Hamming ≤ 6) across all 15,025 AerialWaste and DroneWaste images against every training source. Zero leaks. Both evaluation datasets are held out entirely — every number on this page is zero-shot with respect to them.

Two methods that changed what we can measure

Score the logit margin, not the sampled token. Every earlier number came from parsing generated text, which fixes one operating point and cannot separate "the model doesn't know" from "the model knows and says it at the wrong threshold." Scoring the Yes-vs-No margin at the first answer token and sweeping it is threshold-free and parser-free. It changed the reading of an entire prior section.

Price the calibration. The threshold is one scalar, fit on binary waste/no-waste labels only. It saturates at n ≈ 50 images (93% of a full 3,689-image fit) and transfers across taxonomies within a domain — a cut fitted for the 5-class split applies to the 6-class split at 99.5% of its own-fit value. So the multi-label results stay category-zero-shot, and the protocol to quote is "50 binary labels, one parameter."

The control that makes it credible

DroneWaste was already calibrated, and fitting a threshold there changes nothing (J 0.673 → 0.671). A threshold sweep cannot manufacture separation where the model is not broken — which is why the 2.5–3.6× gain on AerialWaste reads as a fix rather than an artefact of the sweep.

Two things this exposed. n1's open-vocabulary collapse is substitution, not muteness — its "pile" conditioning is −35.3 while "solid waste" is +46.7, because it learned one training corpus's single class name and uses it in place of every material word. Under a supplied menu it names correctly (0.355). The capability is intact; the open vocabulary was overwritten. And s3b describes the scene and misses the waste: fluent, grounded captions about parking lots on images that contain dumping. Lexical diversity is not conditioning, which is why its captions looked good while its detection sat at 0.801.

Recommended stack for open descriptions

1. Decide with the calibrated gate on n2d — one prefill, AUROC 0.9205 / 0.8751, threshold from 50 in-domain binary labels. 2. Describe with n2d's turn-1, only when the gate fires. 3. Do not use the taxonomy commit — it answers "none" on images it has just described as containing debris. It is the broken component, and an open-description product does not need it.

The cost of n2d is over-mention: waste terms on 32.6% of negatives. Gating first is what contains that.

What the VLM is actually doing

Two independent methods, one conclusion. Occlusion: removing the annotated waste — 2.44% of the tile — drops the Yes/No margin by 1.782, against +0.086 for removing an equal area elsewhere (p = 8e-26, 84% of images). Sign flips Yes→No 44 times versus once. Attribution: integrated gradients over the 24×24 token grid put 0.393 of the evidence mass inside the drawn boxes.

That is the measurement the whole architecture rests on: the decoder is a usable training-free localiser and not a namer. Open-vocabulary naming cannot be obtained by varying the question text on this stack. It has to come from a text-aligned encoder — which is what Track C is.

Three caveats that must travel with this table

Fixing both is the same job, and it is inference-only: re-run the two baselines on the 581-image split under both the parser and the margin readout. That is the highest-value remaining spend on this track.

The shared failure mode

Both baselines abstain on most images — empty-parse rates of 53–74%, defaulting to "none". That is the same attractor our own arms kept falling into, in both directions. n1 cuts gate-positive-but-unnamed images to 90 of 581 (15.5%), which is the clearest sense in which this week's work addressed the thing the baselines get wrong. The metrics are related rather than matched, so treat it as directional.

The readout is the ceiling, not the imagery

The most consequential result of the week, and it was nearly missed because it sat in a log file rather than the record. The ceiling experiment crops each annotated object and re-encodes the crop. roi_token_probe.py does the opposite: encode the image once at native resolution, then mean-pool the patch tokens falling inside the box. The readout is a linear probe on frozen features — supervised, so it measures what the features contain, not what the open-world pipeline can currently extract.

This refutes the ceiling conclusion

The ceiling experiment concluded that AerialWaste's five categories are "not recoverable from this imagery — a statement about the task, not the method." That is wrong. A linear probe on ROI-pooled tokens clears the majority bar by 0.266 and predicts all five classes. The information is in the imagery and in the features.

What fails is every text-aligned zero-shot readout — all four sit within ±0.05 of the bar. The honest claim is about the readout: AerialWaste material identity is linearly decodable from frozen features and unreachable by any open-vocabulary text comparison tried.

That prices the architectural constraint. On this dataset, insisting on no fixed label vector costs roughly 0.27 accuracy on the material branch. It is a real cost, and it is a defensible one to pay — but it should be stated as a design choice, not reported as a property of the data.

Which SigLIP2 head, and why one of them cannot work

C-RADIOv4 carries two SigLIP2 heads and they are not interchangeable. _heads.siglip2-g distils the pooled, text-aligned embedding; _feature_projections.siglip2-g distils the patch space, which is not text-aligned. Comparing patch-space vectors against text embeddings is a type error, and it shows:

The dense segmentation arm says the same thing directly: mass-in-mask lift over mask area is 0.0021, i.e. nothing. So the pooled-token naming branch must route through the summary head — which is what roi-head does, and what every routing number on this page uses. Recovering these heads at all required pulling them from the raw checkpoint; the HuggingFace snapshot ships 330 tensors with adaptor_names: null and no teacher heads.

Confidence, routing, and what the decoder cannot do

The encoder knows when it is guessing: entropy separates its correct from its incorrect answers at AUROC 0.817. Deferring the least-confident 30% and re-scoring against only the top-5 candidates lifts that subset from 0.212 to 0.348 and the whole set from 0.478 to 0.519.

Every point of that comes from hand-written prompts. The decoder-written arm — the one job in the design with no non-LLM substitute, since the candidate set does not exist until inference — makes the deferred subset worse than leaving it alone, including in the closed-loop form that finished this week.

The closed-loop arm was the last untested version of this idea: generate 236 candidate prompt sets, keep whichever separates best on the cached image embeddings, using no labels. It beats open-loop (0.161 vs 0.133) and still lands below doing nothing. A decoder writing prompts from names and definitions is worse than not routing, with or without a label-free selection signal.

Worth stating the asymmetry before this hardens into a general claim: the hand-written prompts were written after reading the confusion matrix, so they encode which classes collide. The decoder writes blind. What the result rules out is the open-world configuration, which is the one the architecture actually requires.

Track C · the bound

Detection, measured four ways

The compositional harness separates perception error from reasoning error, which an end-to-end evaluation cannot. 4,496 questions over 759 images, seven families, each scored against its own trivial bar — majority class, or a presence oracle where that is the stronger null.

Reasoning is exact and perception costs 0.538. The solver was verified at 2721/2721 over ground-truth scenes before it was allowed to score anything, which is what licenses attributing the entire gap to the scene graph.

The obvious explanation was over-proposal — 29.3 detections per image against 4.8 real objects. It is wrong. Every box-reducing operation tried made things worse: score filtering, NMS, area filtering, adjacency merging, and overlap merging, five for five. Against the best unconsolidated configuration (0.479, reached at a 0.05 score threshold; 0.462 unfiltered), merging fragments into regions by intersection-over-smaller reaches 0.448, and single-linkage adjacency chains catastrophically — collapsing 86.7 regions into 6.5 — for 0.442. Filtering to the ground truth's own density of 4.1 boxes per image scores 0.404, the worst of all.

What the consistency actually says

Five different operations losing in the same direction is a statement about the query set, not the pipeline. Most families are existential — "is there any A to the left of any B" — so they reward recall and never charge for false positives. The one family where over-prediction genuinely costs is count, and it is pinned at 0.142 against a 0.604 bar in every configuration tried.

This needs fixing before the held-out test run, or the headline number will flatter whichever detector produces the most boxes.

Correction found while assembling this report

Standing caveat on the whole material task

25.8% of AerialWaste's annotated objects are labelled "Unknown material." Annotators working from the full image, at full resolution, with scene context, declined to name a quarter of what they boxed. Whatever a model recovers there is bounded by a target its own authors could not assign a quarter of the time.
